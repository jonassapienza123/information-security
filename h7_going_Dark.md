## Shavers & Bair 2016: Hiding Behind the Keyboard: The Tor Browser €   
- Tor was originally developed in the 1990s by the U.S. Naval Research Laboratory to protect government communications.  
- The project later became open-source and is supported by organizations such as the Electronic Frontier Foundation.  
- Tor is used by journalists, activists, researchers, and people in censored countries to protect privacy and bypass surveillance.  
- Tor works using “onion routing,” where data is encrypted in several layers and sent through multiple network relays.  
- Each relay only knows the previous and next step in the route, which helps hide the user’s identity and location.   
- While Tor improves anonymity, it does not guarantee complete anonymity if users reveal information or misconfigure their systems.  
- Criminals sometimes use Tor to hide their activities on the internet.  
- Law enforcement can still investigate Tor-related crimes using methods such as traffic analysis, undercover operations, and seizing servers.
